Pasadena yard water, asked plainly

My Riviera Beach yard is on a steep bank. Does that change a drainage quote?

+

It can, so describe the bank. About one acre in fifteen of Riviera Beach's land is mapped as Sassafras and Croom soils on 25 to 40 percent slopes, and about one acre in twelve of Lake Shore's land is in soil units on 15 to 25 percent slopes. Say where the water starts, where it ends up and what it crosses on the way.

Has Mountain Road flooded in a summer storm?

+

Yes. On July 8, 2011, NOAA logged flash flooding that closed Mountain Road at Tick Neck Road, about a foot of standing water on Mountain Road at Catherine Avenue, and several inches of water over parts of Mountain Road at Jacobsville. Those are reports about the road. A yard gets its own look, starting with what you saw in the last downpour.

Is my Pasadena yard inside the Critical Area?

+

Only the county's map can say for one lot. About two thirds of Riviera Beach's land and about a third of Lake Shore's land lie inside the Chesapeake Bay Critical Area as the state maps it, the land within 1,000 feet of tidal waters or tidal wetlands. The county has its own rules for work there, so mention how near the shore you are.

How much of Pasadena does the county's sea level rise plan map as flooded?

+

About 68 acres under its 2050 scenario and about 598 acres under its 2100 scenario. That is the flooding the county's 2023 sea level rise plan update maps in the Pasadena community, the largest figures of any community in its table. They are mapped extents for the whole community, not a forecast for one address. A rock channel deals with rain crossing a yard.
