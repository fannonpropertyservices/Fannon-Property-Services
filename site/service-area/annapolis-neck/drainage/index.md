## Near the water, two county rules.

- **Gravel counts as cover** In the Critical Area, gravel, stone and pavers count as lot coverage, just like a driveway, and so may a rock channel.
- **Grading in the Buffer** Grading or filling inside the Buffer needs a buffer management plan, and disturbing more than 5,000 square feet also needs a county grading permit.

## Wet yard questions from the Neck

### Is the soil under my Hillsmere yard the Hillsmere soil on the map?

No. The soil survey names an underwater soil of the South River after Hillsmere Shores, and another underwater soil off the Neck is named for Duvall Creek. Both are mapped under water, not on land, so neither says anything about your lawn; the lie of the lot and where the roof sheds its rain are what a drainage quote looks at.

### What are the stakes along Hillsmere's roads for?

They mark the road curbs and the storm drains. Hillsmere's rules forbid shoveling snow onto the roads and pulling up the stakes that mark curbs and storm drains. If the water in your Hillsmere yard heads for one of those drains, say so when you ask about drainage.

### Can a rock channel keep the Bay out of a Bay Ridge yard?

No. River rock drainage carries rain and roof water off a lawn or away from a drive; it does nothing against tidal water coming in. Shoreline work is a different trade: Hillsmere, for one, has completed a living shoreline at the Sand Spit by its marina; the ribbon cutting was set for September 10, 2026.

### Does Hillsmere's special district tax cover yard drainage?

No. Hillsmere Shores is a county special benefit district, so owners pay an added tax, set per lot, that keeps up its shared property. A rock channel on your own lot is your own project, and our estimate for it is free.
