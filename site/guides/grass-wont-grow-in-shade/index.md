### Why won't grass grow under my trees?

Too little light, and roots taking the water. Turfgrass wants 6 hours of direct summer sun; tall fescue needs at least 4 and fine fescue at least 2, says University of Maryland Extension. Under a maturing canopy a lawn thins year by year, and fall overseeding helps only so far. We overseed where the light allows, around Annapolis.

## Questions about shady lawns

### Which grass seed grows in shade in Maryland?

Fine fescue, and tall fescue where there is a little more light. University of Maryland Extension calls them the two most shade-tolerant turfgrasses: tall fescue tolerates moderate, dappled shade, among the fine fescues Chewings fescue is the most shade-tolerant, and hard fescue is the one that performs strongest in Maryland. Fine fescue takes little foot traffic and should not be mown as often.

### How do I count the hours of sun a spot gets?

Watch it through a day after the trees have leafed out, and note when direct sun lands on the grass and when it leaves. University of Maryland Extension says to track the light over a few days in the growing season.

### Can I cut tree branches to get more sun on the lawn?

Carefully, and not by much. Pruning creates wounds that risk decay, removed branches may not regrow under the upper canopy, and the house loses summer shade. University of Maryland Extension says to use an arborist where possible and to remove no more than a quarter of the canopy in any year.

### What can I plant where grass won't grow under a tree?

Sedges and shade groundcovers, or just mulch. University of Maryland Extension suggests Pennsylvania sedge or bottlebrush grass for shady sites, and a mix of native woodland perennials around a tree's base in place of mulch. A 2 inch layer of organic mulch over the root zone also protects the soil and keeps mowers and trimmers off the trunk.

### Do tree roots stop grass from growing?

They compete for it. Tree roots take moisture and nutrients from the top of the soil and the canopy blocks rain, so the grass beneath runs dry first. University of Maryland Extension says irrigation can help but is not a sustainable fix, because it does nothing about the missing sunlight.
