### How long should water sit in a yard after rain?

As a rule of thumb, give standing water 24 to 36 hours to soak in, unless it is causing new damage, such as water indoors, soil eroding at a foundation, or water over a driveway or patio. If the water is still there after that, check the downspouts, the low spots and how packed the soil is.

## Three places to look.

1. ![Drawing of a downspout that empties at the foot of a house wall, with water lying on the lawn and the soil under it soaked](https://fannonpropertyservices.com/assets/drawn/g13-downspout.svg) **At each downspout.** Roof water can flood the soil where the downspout ends.
2. ![Drawing of a yard cut through the soil, with arrows running down two slopes to water held in a dip](https://fannonpropertyservices.com/assets/drawn/g13-low-spot.svg) **In the low spots.** Water flows from higher ground to lower.
3. ![Drawing of a lawn cut through the soil: water goes down into loose soil at the left, and stays on top of a packed layer under a mower tyre at the right](https://fannonpropertyservices.com/assets/drawn/g13-packed-soil.svg) **Where soil is packed.** Foot traffic, riding mowers and cars pack soil down, and packed soil drains more slowly.

## Questions

### How do I fix standing water in my yard after rain?

Start with where the water comes from. Manage the flow so downspout outlets and other sources do not flood the soil. Where an area still drains poorly, replant it with native perennials or shrubs that tolerate soaked soil.

### Does aeration help with drainage?

It can, when packed soil is the cause. Aerating helps relieve compaction by opening channels for air and water to move through the soil. Aeration alone cannot fix soaked soil.

### Will standing water kill grass?

It can. Soil that collects extra water and drains slowly will stress or kill grass roots. On a lawn, rain should not keep puddling on the soil surface for a day or more.

### When should I get help with a wet yard?

When the water is causing new damage, or is still there after 36 hours. Take photos and video while the water is there, and make notes: where it sits, when the rain stopped and how long it stays. Then call or text (443) 223-8643.

### What if the water is coming from a neighbor's yard?

Talk to the neighbor first. Where a neighbor caused the flooding by redirecting gutter downspouts or blocking a drainage pipe, a one-on-one conversation can often solve it. If that fails, a mediator is an optional next step at no cost or low cost. In Maryland, an upstream owner cannot increase the stormwater flowing onto a downstream owner, and a downstream owner cannot block the natural flow.
