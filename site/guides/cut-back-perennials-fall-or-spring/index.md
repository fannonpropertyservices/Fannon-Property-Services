## Plant by plant, this fall.

- ![Drawing of black-eyed Susan stems left standing in snow with their dark seed heads, a small bird perched on one](https://fannonpropertyservices.com/assets/drawn/cb-susan-standing.svg) **Black-eyed Susan: leave it.** Maryland's state flower keeps its dark seed heads up all winter, and goldfinches feed on them.
- ![Drawing of an ornamental grass clump with its plumes standing in winter, snow at its base](https://fannonpropertyservices.com/assets/drawn/cb-grass-winter.svg) **Ornamental grasses: leave them.** Standing clumps shelter birds, and the insects that eat garden pests, through the cold.
- ![Drawing of a perennial cut to a short stub, its spotted leaves going into a paper yard waste bag](https://fannonpropertyservices.com/assets/drawn/cb-diseased-cut.svg) **Anything diseased: cut it now.** Mildew or leaf spots this year? Cut the stems to about 2 inches once frost has killed them, and bag the debris.

### Should you cut back perennials in the fall or in the spring?

Spring, for healthy plants. University of Maryland Extension says to leave perennials and ornamental grasses standing through winter, then cut them in early spring, before days hold above 50 degrees and bees start flying, leaving 8 to 24 inches of stem. Only diseased plants come down in fall. Spring and fall cleanups are part of our landscape maintenance.

Sources: University of Maryland Extension and the Xerces Society. Checked on 11 October 2026.

## Questions about cutting back

### When do bees start flying in spring in Maryland?

Once daytime temperatures hold above 50 degrees, University of Maryland Extension says, because that is the warmth their flight muscles need. The spring cut should be done before then, so the hollow stubs are standing when bees come looking for nesting sites.

### Which perennials should I leave standing for the birds?

Coneflower, Joe-pye weed, beebalm, mountain-mint and black-eyed Susan. University of Maryland Extension says their seed heads are both attractive in winter and a good food source for birds, and counts them among the exceptions to deadheading after bloom.

### What should I do with perennials that had powdery mildew?

Cut them back in fall once frost has killed the stems, rake out the dead leaves and prune the stems to about 2 inches above the crown. Put the debris out for yard waste collection or in the trash: home compost rarely holds 131 degrees for three days, which is what it takes to kill the fungus.

### Is black-eyed Susan an annual or a perennial?

A short-lived perennial, and sometimes an annual or a biennial. The common Maryland form is Rudbeckia hirta var. pulcherrima, found statewide on roadsides and old fields, blooming June to October. Its seed feeds songbirds, goldfinches above all, which is one reason to leave the heads standing.

### Can the cut stems stay in the yard?

Yes. University of Maryland Extension suggests gathering trimmings into a pile and leaving them outside rather than disposing of them, so overwintering bees and other insects inside can still emerge in spring. Praying mantis egg cases are often fixed to stems too, and are left outdoors to hatch.
