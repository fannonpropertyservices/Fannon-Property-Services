## Questions before you climb

### What kind of ladder do I need to hang Christmas lights?

One tall enough to reach at least 3 feet past the roof edge, and wood or fiberglass rather than metal for outdoor decorating, because metal conducts electricity. Both points come from ESFI, which also says to check the ladder for loose or missing screws, hinges, bolts and nuts before you use it.

### Can I hang Christmas lights without a ladder?

On a single story, often yes. IKO, a roofing maker, describes a light-hanging pole that hooks each clip over the gutter from the ground, and a rented bucket lift as the safe way up to a high peak. Bushes and railings need no height at all.

### Should I get up on the roof to reach the peak?

Not as a homeowner hanging your own lights. IKO's instructions say never to stand or walk on the roof: work from the ladder, and move it often instead of reaching.

### When should I stop and call someone instead?

When the roofline is a second story, when you cannot keep three points of contact on the ladder while clipping, or when nobody can stay with you. We lease and hang Christmas lights from Pasadena down to Edgewater and across the Bay Bridge on Kent Island.

### Should the lights be off overnight if I hang my own?

Yes. ESFI says to turn off all outdoor electrical decorations before leaving home or going to sleep. A timer does it for you, and the Department of Energy's Energy Saver program notes that it saves money and energy too. Every string we install runs on one.

### Do you put up lights I bought myself?

Ask before you count on it. What we quote is a lease: our lights, put up on a timer, fixed during the season, taken down and stored. If all you want is help hanging strings you already own, say so when you send the address and we will tell you whether it fits.
