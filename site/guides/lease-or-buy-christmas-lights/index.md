## Questions about owning or leasing

### Can I buy the lights from you instead of leasing them?

No. What we offer is a lease: the lights stay ours, and we put them up, fix them during the season, bring them down once the New Year is in and keep them in our storage. If owning your Christmas lights matters to you, buying your own strings and hanging them yourself is the better fit.

### Are LED Christmas lights worth paying more for?

Usually, if you plan to keep them for years. The Department of Energy's Energy Saver program says LED lights cost far less to run than incandescent ones, are more durable, last longer and are less of a fire risk.

### How many strings of Christmas lights can I plug together?

For incandescent sets, never more than three, says the CPSC. For any set, the NFPA says to read the maker's instructions for the number of strands that may be connected.

### When should I throw old light strings away?

When a cord is worn or broken or a bulb connection is loose: the NFPA says to replace a string like that. ESFI adds that every light string and extension cord should be inspected for damage before it is used.

### How do I replace a bulb or fuse on lights I own?

Unplug the string first. ESFI says always to unplug electrical decorations before replacing bulbs or fuses, and to keep light strings and extension cords clear of snow and standing water.

### Can indoor Christmas lights go outside?

Not unless the label says so. The NFPA notes that some lights are made for indoor or outdoor use but not both, and ESFI says every cord and decoration used outdoors must be marked for outdoor use and plugged into a GFCI-protected outlet.
